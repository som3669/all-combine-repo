The idea

The European Accessibility Act has applied since 28 June 2025, and the standard behind it — EN 301 549 — points at WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Two questions can now be put to a shop: is it accessible, and can you show what you did about it.

Generic scanners answer neither well. They scan a homepage, report a CSS selector, and treat a checkout like a blog post. AccessProof scans the pages that carry the risk — with a product in the basket, so the real form markup is tested rather than an empty-cart notice — and writes every finding as an instruction rather than a rule name.

A finding, in full

The colour to paste, not a principle

“Darken the text or lighten the background” names no element, no colour and no target. AccessProof measures both colours, the ratio scored, the ratio required, and the nearest shade that passes.

#4f7b0b

Measured 4.1:1, needs 4.5:1. After the change: 4.6:1.

Findings are then grouped by the colour pair behind them, because one colour is reused across a site. On a real store, five colour changes cleared fifty-five failing elements.

Which problems are actually yours

A scan reports plenty that is not your markup at all: WooCommerce’s own block templates, a theme’s mini-cart, WordPress core block output. On that same store, eight of seventeen findings were third-party code.

Every finding carries its origin, and one checkbox narrows the list to work you can actually do. For the rest you get the advice that helps: report it upstream and record it as a known limitation, which is defensible in a way an unexplained failure is not.

What is in it

What the free plugin does

  • Risk-ordered scanning — home, shop, product, cart, checkout, account and blog, with cart and checkout tested with a product in the basket
  • Findings in WooCommerce language — the component named, and the filter or setting to change, rather than a CSS selector
  • Exact contrast fixes — both colours measured, the ratio required, and the nearest passing shade, grouped by colour pair
  • Origin labelling — yours, your theme, WooCommerce, WordPress core, or an installed overlay, with a filter for what you can fix
  • Overlay detection — finds accessiBe, UserWay, Ally, EqualWeb and AudioEye, and reports what the widget has and has not changed
  • Purchase-path ranking — a failure that blocks a sale outranks the same failure on a marketing page
  • Dated scan history — every scan with its score and severity counts, which is the record an auditor asks for
  • A How to use screen — what gets scanned, how to read severity, and plainly what automation cannot check

Every finding opens

The WooCommerce-specific fix, the criterion it fails, the measured colours, the failing element’s own markup, and a link to the technical reference. No working out which button a selector meant.

Not an overlay

Nothing is added for visitors

What an overlay does

  • Adds a floating button and a script from a third-party server.
  • Changes the page only for visitors who find that button and switch something on.
  • Leaves the markup a regulator or auditor tests exactly as it was.
  • In January 2025 the US Federal Trade Commission fined one vendor $1,000,000 for claiming otherwise.

What AccessProof does

  • Finds the failures in your actual source markup.
  • Names where to change them, in WooCommerce’s own vocabulary.
  • Adds nothing to the front end — visitors never load a byte of it.
  • Detects an installed overlay and reports what it has and has not fixed.

Measured on a store with an overlay running and its script loaded: the home page still failed thirteen contrast elements and a focus trap, and one further failure came from the widget itself. Those are attributed to the vendor, because you cannot edit markup a remote script generates.

Questions

Asked before installing

Does this make my store legally compliant?

No tool can promise that, and you should distrust any that does. It finds the machine-detectable failures, tells you how to fix them, and records what you did.

Does it slow my site down?

No. Nothing is added to the front end for visitors. The scan runs only when you click the button, in your own browser.

Where does my scan data go?

Your own database. The plugin makes no outbound connections at all, and page content is never sent anywhere.

Does it work without WooCommerce?

Yes, but the store-specific checks are skipped and you lose most of the value.
